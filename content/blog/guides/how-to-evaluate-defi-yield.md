A DeFi pool can advertise an attractive APY and still be a poor place to put capital. The headline yield tells you what the pool is paying. It does not tell you how durable that yield is, how deep the market is, or how much of the return comes from temporary incentives.

That is why evaluating DeFi yield should start with the quality of the opportunity, not the size of the APY.

## Start with liquidity

Liquidity is one of the first things to check when evaluating a DeFi yield opportunity.

A pool with shallow liquidity can be difficult to enter or exit without meaningful price impact. It can also become more fragile when users withdraw at the same time.

Look beyond the headline TVL. Ask what assets provide that liquidity and whether the market is deep enough for the position you are considering.

Liquidity is not a guarantee of safety. A large pool can still have smart-contract, oracle, governance, or asset risks. But weak liquidity adds another source of risk that should not be ignored.

## Look at APY stability, not just APY

A 100% APY headline is easy to notice. A stable 8% APY can be much more useful.

The important question is how the yield behaves over time.

If APY jumps sharply and then collapses, the headline number may mostly reflect temporary incentives, volatile trading fees, or a short-lived market condition. A lower but persistent yield can provide a clearer picture of what the strategy actually produces.

This is why historical observations matter. One APY reading is a snapshot. A series of readings shows the behavior of the opportunity.

## Separate real yield from incentives

Not all yield is economically equal.

Some pools generate returns from lending interest, trading fees, or staking. Others rely heavily on token emissions and promotional rewards.

Incentive emissions can be useful, but they can also disappear quickly. When the reward program changes, the advertised APY can change with it.

A useful evaluation should therefore ask:

- What generates the return?
- How much depends on emissions?
- Are the rewards liquid and sustainable?
- What happens when incentives decline?

The goal is not to reject incentive-driven pools. It is to understand what you are actually being paid for taking the risk.

## Check the completeness of the data

A missing data point is not the same as a safe data point.

If a pool has incomplete information, the uncertainty itself should be visible. This matters when comparing thousands of opportunities because automated systems can otherwise give incomplete records the same confidence as well-documented ones.

At Litmus, data completeness is one of the signals in the grading methodology. The model does not pretend that missing information is positive information.

## Consider the underlying protocol

Pool-level analysis is only one layer of DeFi research.

Before depositing, investigate the underlying protocol. Look at its contracts, audits, governance, oracle design, dependencies, and history. Also check the specific asset pair or vault strategy rather than assuming that every pool from the same protocol has the same risk.

A grade can help organize research. It cannot replace protocol-level due diligence.

## Use a repeatable framework

The biggest advantage of a structured DeFi yield process is consistency.

Instead of asking, "Which pool has the highest APY today?", ask the same questions for every opportunity:

1. How deep is the liquidity?
2. How stable has the APY been?
3. How much of the reward is incentive-driven?
4. How complete is the available data?
5. What protocol and asset risks sit underneath the pool?
6. Has the opportunity improved or deteriorated over time?

This makes comparisons much more useful.

## Final takeaway

The best DeFi yield opportunity is not automatically the pool with the highest APY.

A better approach combines yield with liquidity, stability, reward quality, data quality, and historical context. That gives you a framework for understanding **why** a pool is offering its return, not just how large the number looks today.

Always verify the underlying protocol and pool before moving funds.
